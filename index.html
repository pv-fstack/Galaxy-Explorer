01 / OVERVIEW

What is a Galaxy?

The Ultimate Cosmic Cities

A galaxy is a massive gravitationally bound system composed of stars, stellar remnants, interstellar gas, cosmic dust, and elusive dark matter. They range in scale from dwarf systems with hundreds of millions of stars to giants harboring one hundred trillion stars.

Our planetary system resides inside the Milky Way — a barred spiral galaxy with sweeping spiral arms glowing with stellar nurseries and cosmic dust lanes.

Galactic Velocity: Our Solar System orbits the supermassive galactic center at approximately 828,000 km/h (514,000 mph), taking 230 million years to complete one revolution.

02 / MORPHOLOGY

Types of Galaxies

Hubble's classification divides galaxies into distinct structural forms. Examine deep-space observations below:

DEEP SPACE OBSERVATION

Spiral Galaxies

Rotating Pinwheel Structure

Characterized by a dense stellar bulge surrounded by a flat rotating disk with sweeping arms rich in interstellar dust and active star formation.

Occurrence: ~60% of Universe Examples: Milky Way, Whirlpool (M51), Andromeda
DEEP SPACE OBSERVATION

Elliptical Galaxies

Smooth Stellar Swarm

Smooth, featureless, ellipsoidal clouds of stars containing mostly ancient population stars with low dust density and minimal active star formation.

Occurrence: ~20% of Universe Examples: M87, IC 1101 (Giant Elliptical)
DEEP SPACE OBSERVATION

Irregular Galaxies

Gravitational Collision Chaos

Galaxies lacking symmetrical structure. Frequently formed from violent gravitational encounters, tidal interactions, or galactic mergers.

Occurrence: ~20% of Universe Examples: Magellanic Clouds, Antennae Galaxies
